WattCast

Forecasts France's hourly electricity consumption one day ahead.

Backtested over 7.5 years and compared hour by hour with RTE's official forecasts.

Try the live demoCode on GitHub

The problem

Electricity can't be stored at scale, so every day RTE forecasts tomorrow's demand, hour by hour. Power plants are scheduled and electricity is traded on that forecast.

WattCast does the same with public data: 11 years of RTE eCO2mix consumption and the temperature of five large French cities.

How it works

  • Day-ahead setup: the forecast for day D is made on D-1, so only consumption up to D-2 is used. A test checks that no future data leaks into the features.
  • Features for calendar effects (French holidays, bridge days, August), thermal inertia of buildings, and recent consumption.
  • Expanding-window backtest: every year from 2019 to 2026 is forecast by a model trained only on the years before it.
  • 80% prediction intervals made reliable with conformal calibration on a held-out year.

What I learned

RTE is still better, honestly

WattCast edges RTE's published forecast, but that series sits 1.1 GW below published consumption since 2023. Corrected for that bias, RTE wins (1.86% vs 2.18%).

Retrain after a shock

A model trained on 2015-2021 and never updated over-forecasts 2023 by about 620 MW: the energy crisis changed consumption.

History can't predict a lockdown

The 10 worst days of the backtest are all from the first COVID lockdown.

Limitations

  • The backtest uses observed temperatures; realistic forecast error adds about half a point of error.
  • National level, temperature only: no wind, sunshine or cloud cover.

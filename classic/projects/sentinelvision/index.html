SentinelVision

Detects and tracks people and vehicles in video, and raises alerts like hazard-zone intrusion.

Runs in real time on a 2-core CPU, with no GPU and no cloud API.

Try the live demoCode on GitHub

The problem

Camera systems in warehouses, shops and car parks produce hours of footage nobody watches. The useful part is a few events: someone entering a hazard zone, loitering, a vehicle stopped where it shouldn't be.

How it works

  • YOLO11 detector exported to ONNX, with pre-processing and box decoding written by hand.
  • A ByteTrack-style multi-object tracker (Kalman filter + Hungarian matching) to follow each person across frames.
  • A rules engine for zone intrusion, loitering, line crossing, crowding and stopped vehicles, with events stored in SQLite.
  • Annotated H.264 video, activity timeline and heatmap for every analysed clip.

Results

MeasureValue
Detector accuracy, YOLO11n on COCO128 (mAP50)0.671 (official evaluator: 0.674)
Speed on a 2-core CPU25 frames per second, up to 1080p
Tracking and rules costunder 1 ms per frame

What I learned

Reproduce the official number first

Matching the official mAP evaluator to within 0.003 proved the hand-written pre-processing and decoding were correct before building anything on top.

Pick speed when it matters

YOLO11n is 2.4 times faster than YOLO11s for 6 points of mAP: the right trade for real time on a CPU.

Limitations

  • No re-identification: a person who leaves and comes back gets a new ID.
  • COCO128 images come from COCO's training set, so absolute mAP is optimistic; it is used to verify the implementation.

Built with

YOLO11, ONNX Runtime, Kalman filter, FastAPI, Streamlit
